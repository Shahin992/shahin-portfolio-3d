The brief

What a website in this industry has to do, and how this one does it.

  1. Answer the first three questions

    Do you help with what I'm going through, what will it cost, and how do I start? A "three quick answers" strip sits right under the hero.

  2. Show fit, not just credentials

    Therapist cards with photo, pronouns, focus areas, format, insurance and whether they're taking new clients, plus filters for focus, age group, format, insurance and availability.

  3. Lower the first step

    A free 15-minute consultation call, and a page that explains exactly what happens before and during the first session.

  4. Open about money and the law

    A full fee table, in-network plans, superbills and sliding-scale spots; a Good Faith Estimate notice for self-pay clients; a privacy notice; forms that never ask for health details; and no advertising pixels.

  5. No testimonials, on purpose

    Counselling and psychology ethics codes bar asking clients for testimonials, so the site says so openly and builds trust with training, process and clear policies instead.

  6. Safety on every page

    A crisis strip with 988, the county crisis line and 911 before every footer, and a quiet "In crisis? Call or text 988" link in the header.

(02)

Design direction

A quiet narrative: one calm, centred column of about 680px that reads like a short book, broken by full-bleed photo "breathers". Photos are masked into soft organic shapes, chips are pills, panels have soft 28px corners and no shadows, and a faint paper grain sits underneath.

Newsreader, a variable serif with optical sizes, sets the headings and pull quotes; Figtree handles the interface. On scroll, the header becomes a floating rounded bar inset from the edges.

Motion. Breathing: slow (0.9–1.4 s) symmetric easing, a fade with a slight rise and blur-to-focus, background shapes that morph slowly and underlines that draw themselves. No bounce and no marquee.

The therapist matcher

(04)

What it does

  1. Three-step therapist matcher

    What's bringing you in, who's coming, and how you'd like to meet and pay. It returns the best-matched clinicians with their next openings, and nothing leaves the browser.

  2. Filterable directory

    Nine clinicians, filtered by focus, age group, format, insurance and availability.

  3. Nine profile pages

    Bio, approach, fees, insurance and hours for each therapist, all generated from one data file.

  4. Specialties

    A pill cloud of concerns linking to specialty pages (anxiety, couples, trauma and PTSD) and to pre-filtered results.

  5. One slow minute

    A 60-second breathing exercise (4 in, 6 out) with a softly expanding shape; text-only with reduced motion.

  6. First-session timeline

    Exactly what happens from the first message to the first session, as numbered steps.

  7. Fees & insurance

    A fee table, in-network plans, superbills, sliding scale, cancellation policy and the Good Faith Estimate notice.

  8. Telehealth

    Video sessions anywhere in Oregon, with a plain explanation of why clients must be in the state during sessions.

  9. Crisis strip

    988, the county crisis line and 911, shown before the footer on every page.

SEO

Ranking in Google

Titles match how people search for care: "Anxiety Therapy in Portland, OR", "Couples Therapy in Portland, OR | Gottman & EFT", "Trauma & PTSD Therapy | EMDR & CPT". Nine clinician profiles carry their credentials in the title, alongside specialty, telehealth and fees pages.

AEO

Answer-first content

43 FAQ answers are marked up, the first session is written as HowTo steps, and the guides answer real questions: "How to Choose a Therapist: 7 Questions Worth Asking" and "Using Insurance for Therapy in Oregon".

GEO

Cited by AI assistants

MedicalClinic, MedicalTherapy and Person schema with an EducationalOccupationalCredential for every clinician, an llms.txt summary and a robots.txt that welcomes 13 AI crawlers by name.

(06)

How it's built

  • Profiles, cards, the directory and the matcher all come from one team.json, so a clinician's fee or insurance list changes in one place.
  • Every photo is cropped from its focal point to WebP, and every page has its own share image.
  • A headless-Chrome responsive audit loads every page at nine screen widths to catch overflow and layout breaks, on top of a static QA pass.
  • Privacy by design: no tracking pixels, and forms never ask for health details.
  • Every page works without JavaScript, and visitors who ask for reduced motion get a calm, static page.

Willowmere FAQ

Who designed and built the Willowmere Therapy Group website?

Shahin Alam, a full-stack software engineer in Dhaka, Bangladesh, designed and built the Willowmere Therapy Group website in 2026: the research, the design, the front-end code, the structured data and the search setup for all 28 pages.

What is the Willowmere website built with?

Hand-written HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript with Motion for animation, generated into 28 static pages by a custom build step. There's no framework and no CMS, and every page works without JavaScript.

What are the main features of the Willowmere website?

Its main features are: Three-step therapist matcher, Filterable directory, Nine profile pages, Specialties and One slow minute.

How is the Willowmere website optimised for Google and AI search?

Every one of its 28 pages has a canonical URL, one h1, an Open Graph image and JSON-LD (MedicalClinic · MedicalTherapy · Person · Service · FAQPage · HowTo). 43 FAQ answers are marked up, the guides are written answer-first, and an llms.txt summary plus a robots.txt that welcomes 13 AI crawlers by name help assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity find and cite it.

Why are there no client reviews on the Willowmere website?

Professional ethics codes for counsellors and psychologists bar asking clients for testimonials. Instead of reviews, the site explains this openly and builds trust with training, memberships, a clear process and transparent fees.

Where can I see the Willowmere website?

It's live at willowmere.shahinkhan.online. It works on phones, tablets and desktops.
