The brief

What a website in this industry has to do, and how this one does it.

  1. Built for the bad night

    People with a broken furnace search on a phone and call the first company that looks trustworthy and available. So the first screen shows a 24/7 status line, the Google rating, the phone number and a ZIP checker with the next arrival windows.

  2. Prices before the visit

    Published flat prices (an $89 tune-up, the diagnostic fee, a 90-day "fixed right" warranty) and a financing calculator that turns an $8,500 system into a monthly figure.

  3. Phones first

    A fixed bottom action bar (Call · Book · Text) and a slide-in menu on phones; on desktop, a Services mega-menu with an $89 tune-up promo card.

  4. Local search

    Service-area pages for nearby towns, a page per service (heating, cooling, plumbing, water heaters) and blog posts that answer real questions, like repair or replace a furnace, or tank versus tankless.

(02)

Design direction

A premium US home-services brand: clean, confident and photo-led. The type is sans-serif only: Archivo, using its width axis for wide, punchy headings, and Public Sans (the US Web Design System face) for body text, with tabular numbers for prices.

Everything sits on a strict 12-column grid with 20px-radius photo frames and frosted "glass" cards floating over the photos: a technician-on-the-way status with a progress bar, a Google rating badge and a thermostat chip. Inner pages open with a navy masthead and a "job ticket" card of quick facts.

Motion. Smooth and confident: 450–700 ms ease-out with no bounce. The header condenses on scroll, counters and prices roll, the process line draws itself and the main button has a soft magnetic pull.

What it does

  1. Heating · Cooling · Plumbing switch

    The hero cycles through three modes until the visitor touches it. The photo crossfades, the headline word changes and the accent moves from heat orange to cool blue to water teal.

  2. ZIP availability checker

    Type a ZIP code to see today's arrival windows for that part of Greater Columbus.

  3. Services explorer

    A list beside a photo panel on desktop, and an accordion on phones.

  4. Kestrel Club membership

    Tune-up plans with a monthly/yearly toggle; the prices roll to the new figure.

  5. Financing calculator

    A project-cost slider plus 24, 60 or 120-month terms give an estimated monthly payment.

  6. Four-step booking

    Service, problem, time window and contact details, one step at a time.

  7. Reviews & service area

    A two-row review marquee that pauses on hover, and a stylised SVG map of the metro with pulsing town dots.

  8. Technicians & promise

    Cards for the people who come to your door, and four written guarantees with a rotating seal.

  9. Answers that rank

    Two FAQ blocks, how-to steps and blog posts written answer-first for Google and AI assistants.

SEO

Ranking in Google

Every page targets one local search: "Furnace & Heat Pump Repair in Columbus, OH", "AC Repair & Replacement", "Water Heater Repair & Replacement". Town pages for Dublin and Westerville, plus a canonical URL, one h1 and an Open Graph image on all 20 pages.

AEO

Answer-first content

50 FAQ answers are marked up as FAQPage and the process as HowTo, and the blog answers the exact question in each title: "Repair or Replace Your Furnace? A Tech's Checklist" and "Tank vs. Tankless Water Heaters: Real Costs in Ohio".

GEO

Cited by AI assistants

HVACBusiness and Plumber schema with service areas and prices as Offer data, an llms.txt summary, and a robots.txt that welcomes 13 AI crawlers by name, including GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot.

(06)

How it's built

  • A custom build step turns one source file per page (with front matter) and shared partials into 20 static pages.
  • Structured data is generated from the visible markup: price rows become Service and Offer, FAQ <details> become FAQPage, and step lists become HowTo, so the schema can't drift from the page.
  • main.js works on its own; motion.js adds Motion 14.3.0 animation by listening for kestrel:* events.
  • 27 photos cropped to WebP, a 1200×630 share image for every page, and variable WOFF2 fonts.
  • Every page works without JavaScript, and visitors who ask for reduced motion get a calm, static page.
